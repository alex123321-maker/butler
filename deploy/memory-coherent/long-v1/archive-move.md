District archive — preparation for a reading-room move
Working memorandum, 18 October 2026
This memorandum describes an invented archive and invented collections.

The archive is moving the public reading room from the east building to the
annex. The collection stores will remain in the east building. An order for
transport trolleys has sometimes been described in conversation as an order to
move the collections. That description is inaccurate: the approved request
covers reader furniture, catalogue terminals and the small reference library.
Collection boxes are to travel individually only after a reader request.

The furniture schedule lists twelve desks, three map tables and two accessible
workstations. The annex floor drawing shows eleven standard desk positions.
The twelfth desk is intended for the staff enquiry point and should not be
counted as an extra reader seat. One map table is still in the conservation
studio. Its release depends on an unrelated surface repair; the move team has
not been told when that repair will finish. The schedule has not been revised.

Network tests on 14 October reached the local catalogue from six annex ports.
Two public terminals authenticated with temporary accounts. The test report
does not include the remote image service, printing or the accessibility
keyboard. A later email says 'network ready', but links only to that local test.
The IT coordinator has asked the sender to list the untested services before
the phrase is reused in the public opening notice.

The temporary booking system allocates a seat number at reservation time. The
old system allocated a desk after arrival. Reservations imported during the
transition consequently have both an old visit number and a new seat number.
Neither number is a box request. The enquiry team plans to retain the mapping
until the last imported visit has occurred. The data protection officer has
not yet approved a longer retention period for that mapping.

For collection Northbank, the old finding aid dates eleven folders to 1910–1916.
A recent conservation note identifies paper watermarks consistent with a later
batch, possibly 1918. The conservator did not redate the correspondence and did
not inspect every sheet. The historical description and the material observation
will be displayed together until a cataloguer evaluates them. The date range
in the finding aid is not being silently replaced by the possible paper date.

Collection Orchard has twenty-nine boxes in the old location list. A shelf
survey found twenty-eight boxes and one empty spacer with the same size and
colour. The missing entry may represent a past numbering change: a 2019 list
has a crossed-out box O-14 and a note 'see O-14a'. The actual contents of O-14a
have not been checked against that note. The survey is an inventory observation,
not a declaration that records were lost or that the historical list was wrong.

The conservation studio can receive at most four large boxes each morning.
That handling limit is different from the reading room's fourteen reader seats.
Readers requesting large maps may use two seats' worth of table space. The
booking team will therefore reserve table capacity separately. An empty seat
in the ordinary calendar cannot guarantee that a large map request can be met.
No transport or conservation promise has been attached to the booking software.

The annex opening was provisionally announced for 2 November. Fire inspection
is booked for 27 October; the appointment confirmation does not report an
inspection outcome. The facilities manager has prepared an alternative plan
using the old room for one additional week. That plan is a contingency draft,
not a cancellation notice. Staff are waiting for the inspection before choosing
which public notice to send to the already booked readers.

Volunteer training has covered the new seat map and the safe trolley route.
It has not covered emergency evacuation from the annex. Two volunteers missed
the practical trolley session and attended only the presentation. The attendance
sheet records those modes separately. A checked attendance box consequently
does not establish that every volunteer can independently perform every task.
The coordinator plans a shorter practical session after the inspection visit.

The final move checklist still needs a service-by-service network report,
the map table's release, a documented treatment of imported reservation mappings,
the Orchard box comparison and the fire inspection outcome. Those items belong
to different teams. The reading-room move does not authorize changing a finding
aid, extending personal-data retention or resolving a conservation hypothesis.
Any condensed planning note should keep proposed dates distinct from completed
checks and leave unsupported historical changes for the responsible cataloguer.
