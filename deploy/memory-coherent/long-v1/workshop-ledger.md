Hillside workshop — maintenance ledger extract
Issued for the planning meeting of 18 October 2026
All people, equipment and events in this document are fictional.

The workshop maintains three types of record: an operator's symptom note, a
technician's measurement sheet, and a release signature. They can refer to the
same machine without having the same evidential meaning. The planning board
used to display only a single status word. Since September it has displayed
three separate columns because parts delivery was repeatedly mistaken for a
completed repair. This extract preserves that distinction.

Lathe Cedar-6 developed a periodic rattle during the evening run on Wednesday.
Operator Ivo wrote that the sound appeared near 900 revolutions per minute and
became quieter after the machine warmed up. He did not record a microphone trace
or bearing temperature. Technician Sena inspected the guard the next morning
and found a loose fastener. Tightening that fastener reduced one vibration in
an unloaded test. The original loaded operating condition was not reproduced.

The parts desk received two bearing sets on Thursday, catalogue references
B-17A and B-17C. The supplier described B-17C as a compatible replacement but
the workshop's approved substitution sheet lists only B-17A. Both sets remain
in sealed packaging. The ledger says that a supervisor asked the supplier for
dimensional drawings; it does not contain a supervisor's substitution approval.
The delivery receipt must not be read as an installation or release record.

For the milling unit Elm-2, the coolant gauge read 2.3 bar at idle and 1.8 bar
during the recorded cut. The manual excerpt attached to the original folder
specifies a range for a different nozzle assembly. The currently installed
nozzle has an unmarked replacement cap. The technician has therefore left the
pressure assessment open rather than applying the old range without checking
the assembly. The pump ran throughout the observation, which establishes
operation during that interval but not adequate coolant flow at every cutter.

A repair appointment was proposed for Saturday morning. The planning board
contains a pencil reservation for a four-hour window. The production manager
has not released the machine for that window, and the technician's leave sheet
shows only two hours of availability. The planner has asked for a shorter
diagnostic visit before scheduling replacement work. No work order has yet
been issued for changing Elm-2's pump or nozzle.

Bench drill Birch-9 passed an insulation check on 3 October. The certificate
names cable harness H-40. On 12 October the operator replaced the detachable
extension lead with a spare, tagged E-12. The machine-side harness appears
unchanged, but the spare lead is outside the named certificate. The ledger
does not say that the old certificate has been cancelled. It also does not
extend the certificate to the spare by association. A separate portable lead
check is requested on the next page, which has not been returned to this file.

The storekeeper counted seventeen belts of size V-34 in bin Seven. The stock
program showed nineteen. One belt had been issued to the training bench and
another had been reserved for an unfinished external order. The issue receipt
exists for the training bench; the reservation has no physical movement record.
The physical count, program balance and available-to-promise figure consequently
describe three different quantities. The clerk kept all three in the meeting
pack so purchasing would not reorder solely from the uncorrected program total.

The workshop trial of a new checklist produced shorter entries but more calls
to the supervisor. Operators said that the checkbox marked 'tested' was unclear:
some meant a brief unloaded run, others meant a full production sample. The
trial team proposed separate checkboxes and a field for the actual conditions.
The proposal has not yet been printed for normal shifts. Older forms remain
valid records of what their authors wrote, despite their narrower vocabulary.

A power interruption occurred at 14:12 on Friday. The cabinet logger stopped
before recording whether the final cut had finished. An operator later found a
part on the discharge tray but could not identify which cycle had produced it.
The team isolated that part for inspection. They did not rerun the cycle merely
because the program displayed its start marker when power returned. The ledger
contains no inspection result for the isolated part as of this edition.

The planner's open items are the loaded rattle test, the actual Elm-2 nozzle
identity, confirmation of the maintenance window, the spare lead check and the
isolated part inspection. These are different dependencies with different
owners. Completing the stock reconciliation would not settle any of the machine
release questions. A useful meeting note should preserve those separations and
should state which supporting records are outside this extract.
