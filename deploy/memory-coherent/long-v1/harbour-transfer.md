Harbour receiving office — transfer notebook
Edition: 18 October 2026, 07:20 local station time
This is a synthetic training document describing a fictional cargo depot.

The receiving office separates the arrival of a crate from acceptance of its
contents. A green arrival tag means that the gate clerk saw a numbered package.
It does not mean that the laboratory has checked the sample inside. The yard
list is maintained by the night dispatcher, whereas the sample register belongs
to the laboratory. Those registers deliberately use different identifiers.

At 05:40 the gate recorded wagon W-318 with seals S-441 and S-442. The driver
carried an old manifest with twenty-four containers. The scale operator counted
twenty-three containers after unloading; one empty return frame was listed as a
container on the driver's sheet. The gate clerk kept both numbers and attached
a short explanation rather than silently adjusting the driver's original.

The laboratory received envelopes L-907 and L-908 at 06:10. L-907 came from the
upper shelf of crate C-18. L-908 came from a reserve crate whose yard label had
become wet. The notebook says that the reserve label was transcribed as C-81;
the photograph, which is not attached to this copy, might instead show C-31.
Nobody has signed a resolution of that discrepancy. The contents of the two
envelopes must therefore remain separate in any later comparison.

The first scale reading was 1,842 kilograms for the loaded pallet. The second
reading was 1,858 kilograms after rain began. The operator noted standing water
on the pallet skirt and did not infer an increase in dry cargo. The tare sheet
for that pallet gives 141 kilograms, but it was last checked with a different
skirt installed. A new dry mass has not been established. A handwritten value
of 1,701 kilograms in a margin is a subtraction, not a certified measurement.

At 06:35 the dispatcher moved C-18 to bay North-2 because the original South-4
door would not latch. South-4 remained on the printed route card. A forklift
receipt establishes the movement into North-2 but has no field for laboratory
acceptance. The receiving office did not ask the forklift driver to settle the
sample identity question. That question stayed with the laboratory supervisor.

Shift note from Mara: the replacement humidity sensor had been installed on
Monday. Its displayed value was 67 percent at 05:50 and 63 percent at 06:45.
The neighbouring old sensor displayed 61 percent throughout the same interval.
The maintenance ticket records installation, not calibration. No conversion
between the two series is justified by the information in this notebook.

The planned inspection order was C-18, C-22, then the reserve crate. In practice
the team inspected C-22 first because its outer packing was torn. They found
three bruised inner trays and placed those trays in a clearly marked holding
area. The other trays were left in the crate. The foreman reported a packaging
problem but did not reject the entire wagon or assign a cause to the damage.

A telephone message at 07:02 asked whether the replacement transport could
leave before noon. The office replied that departure depended on the unresolved
sample identity and the scale recheck. The message log contains no later reply
from the laboratory. A drafted departure sheet was placed in the pending tray;
the document number appears in the log, but no dispatch signature is present.

The receiving clerk uses a blue pencil for unresolved entries. Pencil colour is
an office convention, not a quality classification. Several older pages use red
for the same purpose because the blue pencils were unavailable that week. The
colour of a transcription cannot reliably identify which party raised an issue.

The reserve crate's temporary storage period runs until Tuesday at 16:00. That
period concerns the available bay space. It does not set the age limit for a
sample result and does not authorize disposal of material. The laboratory has
its own retention schedule, which is absent from this extract. The dispatcher
has requested that schedule and has recorded the request as still unanswered.

Items carried to the next shift are the unresolved reserve label, a dry scale
reading with the correct tare, and the laboratory's disposition of L-907 and
L-908. The next clerk is expected to preserve the original sheets alongside any
correction. A later typed inventory should distinguish what was physically seen,
what was calculated, and what is still waiting for an independent check.
