Meadow pumping station — operational round and planning notes
Compiled at the handover on 18 October 2026
The station, readings and staff described below are entirely fictional.

Pump P-3 serves the hill reservoir through a long rising main. Pump P-4 serves
the nearby industrial loop. The shared control panel shows both machines on
one screen, which led a visiting contractor to refer to them as a duty and
standby pair. The pipe diagram does not support that arrangement. Moving a
setpoint from one pump to the other would affect a different service area.
The shift notebook therefore retains the machine and loop with each reading.

At 04:50 P-3's outlet gauge showed 5.1 bar while the reservoir telemetry showed
62 percent. At 05:20 the same gauge showed 4.9 bar and the telemetry showed
64 percent. The operator noted a street-cleaning draw from the lower branch
between those readings. There is no simultaneous flow series in this extract.
The two pressures alone cannot distinguish changing demand, gauge error and
other causes of the small difference.

The reservoir level transmitter was cleaned on Tuesday. A maintenance entry
says that sediment was removed from the stilling tube. The entry contains no
before-and-after reference measurement. The operating team has not designated
the cleaning as calibration. Until a comparison with the staff gauge is done,
the transmitter series is retained with its existing uncertainty annotation.
A fresh display timestamp shows transmission, not independent level accuracy.

P-4 stopped for seven minutes at 06:03 after a local overload indication. The
electrical team reset the protection after an inspection of the cabinet. Their
note says that no visible damage was found; it does not state why protection
operated. The pump restarted and its motor current remained within the displayed
range during a ten-minute observation. That observation is narrower than a
complete diagnosis of the earlier stop or a long-term reliability assurance.

The planning office has received an estimate for replacing the ageing outlet
gauge. The estimate includes two gauges of different pressure ranges because
the storekeeper requested a spare for a second station. Only one item belongs
to Meadow. The purchasing record has not been approved. An emailed delivery
date of 24 October is conditional on an order being placed by Monday noon.
Neither the estimate nor that date establishes that a replacement is on site.

A noise survey near the residential boundary compared a normal morning round
with a Sunday afternoon. The report gives 49 and 44 decibels respectively, but
the microphone positions differed by five metres and the Sunday sheet notes
wind. The contractor did not provide a background correction. The residents'
liaison officer has asked for a repeat at the same marked position before
attributing the difference to any particular operating change.

The spare-parts shelf contains a seal kit tagged for P-3. Its outside bag is
intact, but the kit's inventory list names an older impeller housing. The
maintenance lead has requested the manufacturer's drawing to check fit. The
technician has not opened the bag or fitted the seal. The stock system's
'available' state means that the bag is physically on the shelf; it is not a
technical determination that every component matches the installed machine.

During the previous round a contractor asked whether the industrial loop could
be isolated at 11:00. The notebook records the request and the operator's reply
that the food plant needed notice. There is no notice acknowledgement in the
handover folder. The valve exercise appears on the draft work plan but has no
authorized isolation window. The incoming shift must check the separate permit
register instead of treating a planning conversation as an operating permit.

Weather information in the folder consists of a forecast downloaded on Friday
and a rain gauge reading written early this morning. The forecast predicts a
short dry period on Sunday. The gauge records seven millimetres since its last
emptying, whose time is not specified. Those records answer different questions;
the accumulated amount cannot verify the forecast's timing or establish when
the recorded rain actually fell.

The next shift's requested follow-up is a staff-gauge comparison, the overload
investigation status, the conditional gauge order, the repeat noise survey and
the isolation permit check. All remain separate. No instruction in this extract
changes a pump setpoint or grants an isolation. A planning summary should retain
the loop identities, the actual observation conditions and the missing records
so the responsible teams can decide what to inspect next.
